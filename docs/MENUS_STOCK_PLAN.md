# Menus stock: ingredients are the smallest thing (no products behind them)

Status: **steps 1 to 5 built** (see Build notes at the end); step 6 (cleanup of the old product-based code, new sample data) is next. Written after the owner's decision: Menus will not depend on products or on the E-commerce module, because products are moving out of core into E-commerce later. This replaces the current design, where an ingredient is a group of product variants and a dish owns a hidden product.

## What the owner decided
- **An ingredient is the lowest thing.** "Wheat flour". It has a **base unit** (g) and an **alternate unit** (kg), both from the same unit group in the units table, so conversions come from that table.
- **An ingredient has variants.** Exe, Pembe, Soko. A variant uses the ingredient's units.
- **Purchasing** buys ingredient variants (in the base or the alternate unit).
- **Stock is calculated per ingredient variant.** Making a dish reduces the variants themselves. **Wastage** is recorded against the variant.
- **No hidden products, no product tables** anywhere in Menus.

## What is true today (why this is a real change)
- An ingredient family points at product variants (`menus_ingredient_variants.variant_id`), a recipe line points at a family or a product variant, a dish owns a hidden product (`DishItems`), and every stock movement goes through the product stock engine (`BatchService`, `variant_location_stock`, `StockWriteOffService`, counts, transfers, expiry).
- Voucher lines of type `product` are refused when the E-commerce module is off (`VoucherService::assertLineModuleOn`), so today a Menus-only business could not even sell a dish on a voucher.
- Groundwork exists: script 49 gave `stock_movements` and `stock_batches` an `item_type` and `item_id`, and `StockItemRegistry` lets a module register its own kind of stocked item for the reports. The engine itself (`BatchService` and the rest) still works on product variants.

## The design

**Menus owns its stock.** New Menus tables; nothing in Menus reads `products`, `product_variants` or `variant_location_stock`.

| Table | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `menus_ingredients` | the ingredient: name, **base_unit_id**, **alt_unit_id** (same dimension, checked), allergens, tags, how the kitchen picks a variant, reorder level, active. Grows out of `menus_ingredient_families` (its `unit_id` becomes the base unit). |
| `menus_ingredient_items` | the **variant**: ingredient, name (Exe), code, optional pack size (2 kg), preference order, allowed as a substitute, expires yes/no, default buying price. Replaces `menus_ingredient_variants`. |
| `menus_stock_batches` | received lots of a variant at a branch: quantity in the **base unit**, left, unit cost, batch no, expiry, held or recalled. |
| `menus_stock_moves` | every change: purchase, opening, consumed by a dish or a production run, waste, count correction, transfer, with the voucher or run it came from. |

Quantities are always kept in the base unit. Buying "10 kg" stores 10,000 g; screens show whichever unit the person prefers. First-expiring-first and the pick rule (preferred, cheapest, first to expire) work as now.

**Purchasing.** Purchase and goods-received vouchers get a line type **ingredient** (allowed when Menus is active), with a variant, a quantity in the base or alternate unit, a price, a batch and an expiry. Posting receives the stock and the journal is Dr Ingredient stock, Cr the supplier (and input tax as usual). Opening stock for ingredients works the same way.

**Using ingredients.** A recipe line names an **ingredient** and an amount in its base or alternate unit, with waste. A sale of a made-to-order dish, or a production run, asks the ingredient's pick rule which variants to take from, reduces them batch by batch, and records the cost. Cost of food is posted at the batch cost: Dr Cost of food, Cr Ingredient stock.

**Dishes without products.** A dish is sold as a new voucher line type **dish** (Menus module), not as a product. A dish that is **made ahead** (the cake) has its own small stock of finished portions in the same Menus tables, filled by a production run that consumes the ingredients and costs the batch.

**Wastage.** The waste log writes off an ingredient variant (or a dish, which writes off its ingredients) with a reason; the journal is Dr Food wastage, Cr Ingredient stock.

**Counts, transfers, expiry, reorder, prep list.** All re-pointed at the ingredient variants: a stock count of ingredients, transfers between branches, the expiring list, the reorder suggestions (which raise a purchase order for ingredient variants), and the prep list.

**Ledgers.** Menus Configuration gets three accounts: Ingredient stock (asset), Cost of food (expense), Food wastage (expense). The defaults come from the existing Stock in hand, Cost of sales and Stock loss accounts so nothing needs setting up first.

## Steps (each tested and pushed on its own)
1. **Ingredients and variants.** Script and screens: create an ingredient with base and alternate unit (alternate limited to the same unit group), add variants. No stock yet.
2. **Stock in.** The ingredient line on purchase and goods-received vouchers, opening stock, the ingredient stock screen per branch, the journals.
3. **Using stock.** Recipes on ingredients, the pick rule over variants, consumption by a sale and by a production run, cost of food, availability ("sold out when an ingredient cannot cover one more portion").
4. **Dishes sold without products.** The `dish` voucher line, the till, the kiosk, orders and meal plans moved onto it; made-ahead stock.
5. **Wastage, counts, transfers, expiry, reorder, prep list.**
6. **Tests and mutation checks for all of it**, and a new sample-data guide.

The Menus tests that build products today (dishes, counter, kitchen, orders, reorder, waste, prep) are rewritten as each step lands.

## Not needed
- **No migration** of ingredient or recipe data: Menus has only been tried with test data. The old ingredient tables are left alone until the owner says to drop them.

## Decisions (the owner's answers)
1. **Menus' own tables.** Yes.
2. **Dishes leave products too** (the `dish` line). Yes.
3. **Pack sizes.** Yes, and it is for stock taking: a variant "Exe 2 kg" has a pack size of 2 kg; the count screen asks "how many packs" (10 packs is 20 kg); another variant "Exe 10 kg" with 3 packs is 30 kg. Stock is stored in the base unit; the count and purchase screens take packs, the alternate unit or the base unit.
4. **Ledgers.** Named as the owner's list: **Food Ingredients Inventory** (current asset), **Food Cost of Sales** (direct expense), **Food Waste Expense** (direct expense). Suppliers on a purchase are the ledgers under the **Sundry Creditors** group.
5. **Branches.** Each branch has its own stock.

## Build notes
- Step 1 is built as new tables beside the old ones (script 181: `menus_ingredients`, `menus_ingredient_items`) with their own screen and API (`/admin/menus/stock/ingredients`). The old `menus_ingredient_families` and `menus_ingredient_variants` stay until recipes are moved onto the new ingredients in step 3; until then the Recipes screen still reads the old ones, so Ingredients and Recipes are not meant to be tried together before step 3.
- A variant's pack size is kept in the base unit and may be typed in the alternate unit.

- Step 2 is built (script 182): `menus_receipts`, `menus_receipt_lines`, `menus_stock_batches`, `menus_stock_moves`, and the three accounts. **How it posts:** receiving ingredients creates an ordinary **Purchase voucher** whose lines are plain ("custom") lines posted to Food Ingredients Inventory, with the supplier (a ledger under Sundry Creditors) credited, so payments, ageing and tax work as for any supplier bill; the stock goes onto Menus' own shelf in the same step, in the base unit, costed per base unit before tax. The voucher names its receipt (`meta.menus_receipt_id`): Books can **cancel** it (which takes the stock back, and is refused naming the item if some has been used) but cannot **edit** it or **return** it. Opening stock is a Journal Dr Food Ingredients Inventory, Cr the opening balance account, with the same shelf. Quantities are typed in packs (when the variant has a pack size), the alternate unit or the base unit.
- The screen is Menus, Stock (receive, opening stock, what is on the shelf per variant in packs, the bigger unit and the base unit, receipts with cancel). Menus configuration has the three account selects.

- Step 3 is built (script 183): recipes written on ingredients (`menus_recipes`, `menus_recipe_lines`, `menus_recipe_steps`, keyed by dish), `menus_uses` (one record each time stock is used, with its cost and the journal that booked it) and `use_id` on the stock moves. `IngredientUse` is the engine: **needs** (amounts scaled to the portions, waste added back as qty / (1 - waste)), **allocate** (the pick rule: preferred = variant order then soonest expiry; cheapest = lowest batch cost; first_expiring = soonest expiry anywhere; expired, held, recalled, not-allowed and switched-off stock never used; all or nothing), **capacity** (portions the shelf can make), **cost** (average of the shelf, else the last cost; incomplete when something was never bought), **consume** (batches down, a move per batch, Dr Food Cost of Sales / Cr Food Ingredients Inventory at batch cost) and **reverse** (the same batches get their quantity back, the journal is cancelled, safe to call twice). A dish's allergens, diets, portions and cost now come from its new recipe when it has one. **Still to come in step 4:** a sale calling `consume`/`reverse` (through a dish line on the voucher and a thin hook, like the rewards hooks), the dish's own price, and made-ahead batches with their own dish stock.

- Step 4 is built (script 184): a dish has its own `price` and no product. A `dish` voucher line (like a custom line, booked to the Menus sales account, tax by account) is the way a dish is sold; two thin hooks in `VoucherService` (`persist` applies, `reverseEffects` undoes) call `DishStock`, which uses the stock for vouchers that move stock out (an invoice, a cash sale, a delivery note) and gives it back on edit or cancel. One journal per voucher: Dr Food Cost of Sales, Cr Food Ingredients Inventory at batch cost. A choice on a dish (leave out onion, extra cheese) changes the ingredients used. A dish **made ahead** has its own batches (`menus_dish_batches`) filled by a production run (`DishProduction`: ingredients used, nothing booked until the portions are sold, because the value stays inside the inventory account). Till, kiosk, orders, kitchen screens and meal plans now run on dishes.
- Step 5 is built (script 185): **waste** names an ingredient variant (typed in packs, the bigger unit or the base unit) or a dish (a dish made to order writes off its ingredients, one made ahead writes off its own portions); lots leave first-expiring first, expired and held lots included; journal Dr Food Waste Expense, Cr Food Ingredients Inventory; taking an entry back returns the exact lots and cancels the journal. **Stock taking** (`StockCounts`): count in packs; a shortage comes off the lots, stock found becomes a lot at the last known cost; the net is booked to Food Waste Expense. **Transfers** between branches (`StockTransfers`) keep each lot's cost, expiry and batch number. **Expiry list** (`IngredientStock::expiring`). **Prep list** and **reorder** now read recipes and the shelf; a variant can carry a *level to keep* (`menus_ingredient_items.reorder_level`, base unit); suggestions are in whole packs of the preferred variant (else the bigger unit, else the base unit) and "make orders" raises one Purchase Order per supplier (the supplier is whoever a variant was last received from).

- **Step 6 (script 187): ingredients on the core Purchases pages.** The kitchen's stock now takes part in everything under Purchases. *Vouchers:* a Purchase, Goods received, Opening stock or Purchase Order voucher can have **ingredient lines** (item = the ingredient's name, variant = the variant's name, quantity typed in packs, the bigger unit or the base unit, `unit_factor`/`base_quantity` kept like a product's, rate = price of one of that unit); the line posts to Food Ingredients Inventory and, after the voucher is saved (`IngredientVoucherStock`), the lot goes on Menus' shelf; cancelling or editing the voucher takes it off again (refused when some was used). Receiving from Menus makes a Purchase voucher; Menus opening stock makes one Opening stock voucher **per variant**. *Journal and reports:* every `menus_stock_moves` row is mirrored (`StockMoveObserver`) into `stock_movements` as `item_type = menus_ingredient_item`, so the stock journal and stock reports see purchases, uses, waste, counts and transfers; script 187 copies the earlier ones in. *Counts:* a core stock count also lists the ingredient variants (`stock_count_ingredient_lines`), counted in packs, the bigger unit or the base unit; posting makes a second Journal (Food Waste Expense / Food Ingredients Inventory) kept on `stock_counts.ingredient_voucher_id`; Menus' "Count stock" makes a core count and posts it at once. *Transfers:* core transfers carry ingredient lots (`stock_transfer_ingredient_lines`): in transit, received (a short delivery is written off to Food Waste Expense), or cancelled; Menus' "Send to a branch" is a core transfer sent and received at once. *Expiry:* the expiring list includes ingredient lots and can write them off as food waste. *Screens:* item pickers on Purchases, Transfers and Counts search products when E-commerce is on and ingredients when Menus is on ("Search an ingredient", "Search a product or SKU", "Search an ingredient or product"); Held stock and Jobs in progress show only with E-commerce.
- **Step 7: debit notes and purchase orders take ingredients; sales documents do not.** Ingredients are bought, not sold: invoices, cash sales, delivery notes, sales orders, quotations and credit notes refuse them (the message says to make a dish from it and sell the dish). A **purchase order** can have ingredient lines (Books voucher form: "Ingredient" line, packs, bigger unit or base unit; the reorder suggestions raise POs with them) and converting it to goods received or a purchase keeps the variant and unit and puts on the shelf only what is received. A **debit note** is made against a purchase like a product's: pick the ingredient lines and how much goes back (return, adjust the price, or write off). Returned quantity leaves the lots that purchase (or its goods received note or order) brought in, first-expiring first, at the lots' cost, refused saying how much is left when some has been used; cancelling the note puts it back. A purchase cannot be cancelled while a debit note against it holds stock out of its lots (cancel the note first).
- **Step 8 (script 189): ingredients are bought under a purchase account.** The account decides the VAT on a purchase, as a product's does; the stock is still carried on Food Ingredients Inventory and costed before tax. The account is the ingredient's own (`menus_ingredients.purchase_ledger_id`), else **Purchase account for ingredients** in Menus, Configuration, else the voucher type's default, else the books' default purchase account. The Purchases form, Receive ingredients, purchase orders and goods received all use it (the manual tax choice on Receive ingredients is gone); opening stock is never taxed; a debit note reverses the tax of the purchase line. The script also adds **Purchases - exempt** and **Purchases - zero rated** beside Purchases.
